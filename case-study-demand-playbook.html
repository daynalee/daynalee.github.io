Turning Around an Underperforming UA Campaign

A five step diagnostic I run before changing a single setting. Most underperforming campaigns are misdiagnosed, so this playbook finds the real problem first: measurement, funnel, creative, audience, or auction.

Role: Client Partner
Team: Advertiser Growth
Format: Playbook
Partners: Advertisers, Programmatic Team, Creative
Situation
An advertiser's UA campaign is missing its CPI or ROAS target and nobody can agree on why.

Overview

When a campaign underperforms, the instinct is to act: raise bids, swap creative, widen targeting. The problem is that underperformance is usually misdiagnosed. Sometimes the numbers themselves are wrong, an attribution lag or a broken postback that looks like a performance drop. Other times the fix sits in a different part of the funnel than where the symptom shows up.

This playbook is the order I check things in, refined across the advertiser accounts I manage. It is a methodology rather than a single client story, so there are no cherry picked results here.

Context

Problem Statement

When a campaign misses target, everyone has a theory. The advertiser blames the inventory, the platform team blames the creative, the creative team blames the targeting. Without a shared diagnostic order, teams change three things at once and learn nothing.

My Role

As the client partner I own the diagnosis. I pull the funnel data, run the checks below, and bring the advertiser one clear read: what is actually wrong, and what we are changing first.

When To Use This

  • CPI drifting above target for five or more days
  • Sudden performance drops with no obvious cause
  • New campaigns that never hit target after launch
The Five Steps

1. Verify measurement first

Before anything else: check attribution windows and SKAN postback delays, compare platform reporting against the MMP, and confirm tracking survived any recent SDK or app update. A meaningful share of "performance drops" I have seen were measurement artifacts, not real drops. Fixing a tracking issue takes an hour; rebuilding a campaign around bad data takes weeks.

2. Read the funnel top down

Delivery, CTR, install rate, post install quality. Where the drop sits tells you the lever. Delivery problems point to budget, bid, or audience. CTR problems point to creative or placement. Post install problems point to targeting or the landing experience. The symptom and the cause are rarely in the same place.

3. Separate creative, audience, and auction

These three get confused constantly, and each has a different fix:

  • Creative fatigue: frequency climbing while CTR falls on the same audience. Fix is creative, not budget.
  • Audience saturation: CPM rising, reach flattening, new user share dropping. Fix is audience expansion or refresh.
  • Auction pressure: clearing prices up with no change in your setup. Fix is bid strategy or timing, ideally with programmatic input.

4. Check incrementality

Are we buying users the advertiser would have acquired anyway? Look for overlap between campaigns, branded search lift during campaign flights, and shifts in the organic baseline. Spend that cannibalizes organic installs looks like performance until you net it out.

5. Change one thing, with a kill criteria

Write down the hypothesis, the single change, what success looks like, and the date we revert if it does not work. No stacked changes. The log becomes the QBR story: here is what we tried, what we learned, and what we did next.

Challenges
  • Pressure to act fast: advertisers want the fix today, and the diagnostic takes a day or two. I frame it plainly: one day of diagnosis saves two weeks of guessing.
  • Stacked variables: the hardest part is resisting the urge to change bid, budget, and creative together. One change at a time is slower to start and faster to finish.
  • Honest limits: this playbook finds the lever. It does not replace creative testing volume or deep auction level analysis. For the latter I partner with our programmatic team rather than doing hands on trading myself.
Results

The win here is process, not a single heroic number. What the advertiser gets from a run of this playbook:

  • A clear diagnosis within days, in language the whole team shares
  • One targeted change instead of scattershot edits
  • A written record that makes the next QBR easy to tell
  • A repeatable process the team can run without me in the room
