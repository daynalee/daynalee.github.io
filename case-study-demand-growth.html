Finding the Real Problem

One of my T1 publishers had a steady decline in revenue. Nothing in their monetization setup explained it, so I kept working backwards until I found it. Their DAU was falling. Fewer players, less inventory, less revenue.

Previously they'd told me they weren't interested in running UA with us. They were already doing it on other networks and it was working for them. The DAU finding gave me a reason to bring it up again, and this time the answer was different: those campaigns had stopped performing and they were pulling spend back.

My role: I own both sides of this relationship. I ran the diagnosis, made the case for UA, handled the objections, and I still own the weekly recommendation cycle.

The Opening

Every time I'd raised UA before, the answer was no. This time they had a reason to listen.

Before going back to them I went through the whole account looking for where demand could actually work: which titles had room, where their supply numbers pointed to an audience worth buying, and what a realistic starting point looked like. Then I pitched it.

Why they hesitated: they hadn't heard about our recent updates and hadn't seen our performance improvements. As far as they knew, nothing had changed on our side since the last time they'd looked at us.

How I Got the Yes

I didn't try to talk them round on the spot. I asked them to give me every concern they had, wrote them all down, and went away to work through them.

I put together a specific answer for each one and booked the next meeting in person. It was a long list, and I wanted to be in the room for it.

In that meeting we went through:

  • Every concern they'd raised, answered one at a time
  • What had worked and what hadn't on their other networks
  • What they actually wanted out of UA, which was not the same as what the previous networks had been optimizing for
  • Their real limitations on budget, creative and headcount

They agreed to $300 a day. On demand spend alone that number doesn't earn an in person meeting. On the whole relationship, they're one of the biggest partners I have.

Getting Them Live

Audited the setup and the launch strategy

Performance Ops got them live, and I went over it again before spend ramped. Attribution and event mapping, bid strategy and target ROAS, budget headroom, geo and platform splits, blocklists, and the launch plan itself. Small budgets fail on setup far more often than on strategy, and a bad setup doesn't announce itself. You just see soft numbers three weeks later and start guessing at causes.

Went through our best practices in detail

They didn't have a UA function, so I wasn't going to hand over a deck and leave them to it. We went through it properly. How tROAS relates to observed ROAS, and why delivery stalls once that gap gets too wide. Why a D0 campaign lets you adjust every three days while a D28 campaign makes you wait. When to widen geos instead of reaching for the bid.

Brought creative ideas instead of a creative ask

Their creative was cut for their own store listing and it didn't translate to our placements. We went through what actually works on our inventory: playables that hand you a real piece of the game instead of a two tap demo, a top scaling video paired with an interactive end card, and a refresh cadence you set in advance rather than waiting for fatigue to turn up in the numbers.

Kept iterating on the strategy

We picked the launch titles, the campaign structure and the ROAS targets together, then revised them as results came in. Most of what they run today looks nothing like what we launched with.

The Weekly Tracker

Once they were live, everything ran through one shared sheet. They fill their half by Monday and we fill ours before the call.

Issue type + priorityClientPerformance, scale, or creative, ranked by urgency and revenue impact
Campaign + detailsClientThe specific campaign and what they are seeing
Best practices followedClientA true or false gate. If it is false we fix the setup before making any performance judgments
RecommendationUnitySpecific recs for the client to action themselves in our self serve dashboard
Monitoring notesBothWhat happened after it went live, including when it did and didn't work
Status + last updatedBothOpen, in progress, or resolved

The client fills in that best practices column themselves, so the check sits with them rather than with me.

Without a record, every review turns into a debate over whether the advice was ever followed. With one, we skip that and talk about what we learned. It works against me too, since the recommendations that flopped are sitting right there, and being straight about those is most of why they trust the ones that land.

Then We Scaled

With the setup clean and the cadence running, scaling was mostly repetition. Every title got a campaign of its own, a target, and a line in the tracker, and whatever worked on one became the starting point for the next.

Plenty of it didn't work. One title held 41% D7 ROAS at $125 a day and dropped to the low twenties when we pushed it to $300, so we pulled it back instead of buying volume at a loss. Another we paused outright and relaunched on a longer ROAS window.

They're at $5.7K a day now across a growing list of titles.

Challenges
  • Selling a channel that had just failed them: the objection wasn't about us, it was about UA. Answering it meant separating what our platform does now from what they'd experienced elsewhere, concern by concern.
  • Justifying the time: nothing about $300 a day says block out a day for an in person meeting. I looked at the entire relationship and the potential rather than just the revenue and spend.
  • No UA team on their side: every recommendation had to be teachable, not just right. Slower up front, much faster after.
  • Patience at small scale: numbers on a $300 a day test are noisy, and the hard part was leaving the setup alone long enough to actually read them.
  • Where I stop: bid tuning and auction level work sit with our programmatic team. I own the relationship, the strategy, the onboarding and the weekly cycle.
Results
  • $300 a day to $5.7K a day, roughly 19x, and still climbing
  • A client who was pulling out of UA entirely now running it as a standing channel across multiple titles
  • New titles come to us first now, so the growth compounds without a fresh pitch each time
  • Their DAU problem getting solved on the demand side, which protects the supply revenue underneath it
