Advertiser Growth

Growing a Demand Book Past a Saturated Anchor Title

An advertiser in my book had one title carrying nearly all their spend, and it had saturated. I moved budget to a second title and changed how they measured return. Record spend quarter, with cost per install down and ROAS up.

Situation
One title carried nearly all the advertiser's spend and had saturated. eCPM had tripled. Nowhere left to scale.
Action
Broke performance out per title, proved the creative was fine and the audience was gone, moved budget to a second title, and lengthened the ROAS window.
Result
Record spend quarter. Second title scaled 6x while holding efficiency. CPI down, D7 ROAS up.
The Problem

The account looked healthy. Spend was steady, the client was happy. Almost all of it sat on one flagship title that had saturated its audience, and eCPM on it had tripled. Every extra dollar was buying worse users.

Steady spend on a saturating title is a cliff with a flat approach, and my job was to see it before they felt it.

What made it hard: they had no reason to think anything was wrong, the second title was unproven, and their measurement window was too short to show the value of the users it would bring in.

My role: relationship and growth plan. I am not placing bids day to day. I bring the diagnosis and get the recommendation implemented, which is usually the harder half.

How I Run the Account

A standing weekly call with the advertiser's UA team, plus a QBR. Same four parts every week.

1. What changed, per title

Delivery, pacing, CPI, and ROAS against target, broken out by title rather than blended. Blended numbers hid exactly this problem: a saturating title and a scaling title average out to fine.

2. The recommendation tracker

Every recommendation goes into a shared tracker:

ColumnWhat it does
RecommendationWritten specifically enough to action without me in the room
Owner + target dateKeeps it from dying in a meeting note
Best practice checkBid strategy, event mapping, attribution window, creative refresh rate, budget headroom. Confirms the setup is right before we layer anything on top
Monitoring notesWhat happened after launch, and whether we kept it

Without it, every QBR is a debate about whether advice was followed. With it, the record is there and the conversation moves to what we learned. It cuts both ways: a recommendation that failed is visible too.

3. Creative breakdowns

I break performance out by concept, format, and hook. I am separating fatigue from a bad concept, which look identical in aggregate and have opposite fixes. Fatigue is frequency climbing while CTR falls on the same audience, and the fix is new executions. A bad concept never worked on any audience, and the fix is a different idea.

Here the breakdown showed the flagship's creative still converted. The audience had run out. That is what pointed to a second title instead of a creative refresh, and it saved them a production cycle they were about to spend.

4. One change, with a revert date

Each week closes with a single change, a success criteria, and a date we undo it. Stacked changes teach you nothing.

What I Changed

Moved budget to a second title

Instead of pushing more into a saturated audience, incremental spend went to a title in their portfolio with a genuinely different player base. It scaled about 6x over the quarter and held efficiency. Scaling while efficiency collapses is not growth.

Lengthened the ROAS window

Their optimization ran on a short return window that undervalued the users the new title brought in, because those players monetized later. We switched mid quarter. On the first full cohort measured the new way, CPI came down about 7% and D7 ROAS improved about 2.8 points.

Wound the flagship down deliberately

Down to the spend level where it was still efficient, headroom moved elsewhere. Advertisers rarely resist growth. They resist being told to stop doing the thing that used to work, which is why the creative breakdown had to land first.

Challenges
  • Selling a problem they could not see: the account was performing. The per title breakdown made saturation legible before I proposed a fix.
  • Measurement change is a trust ask: a longer ROAS window means worse looking numbers for a few weeks before the picture corrects. That only works if the tracker has already shown my calls tend to hold up.
  • Where I stop: auction level work and bid tuning ran with our programmatic team. Mine was the diagnosis, the plan, and the implementation schedule.
Results
  • Highest spend quarter on record, with growth coming from a newly scaled title rather than from pushing a saturated one harder
  • Second title scaled roughly 6x while holding efficiency
  • CPI down about 7%, D7 ROAS up about 2.8 points on the first cohort under the new window
  • A written recommendation record that made their first ever QBR straightforward to build and easy to defend
